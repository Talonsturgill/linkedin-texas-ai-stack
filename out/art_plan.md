# Art plan

## What happened

NSF announced a UT Austin-led AI and robotics center whose public five-year total is delivered
through a cooperative agreement with annual obligations, reports, and performance review.

## Why it matters to Texas

Austin controls the center budget and partner subawards, but the next federal increment remains
behind an annual evidence gate.

## Register

Precise, consequential, and quietly optimistic. The image should feel like a museum object built
to explain control, not a warning poster.

## Three concepts

### Five-year stack, one live gate

Five translucent resin wafers float in a vertical mechanism. The first wafer glows as funded.
The later wafers remain cool and dim. A small amber aperture between the first and second holds a
physical report folio, making the annual evidence gate visible in half a second.

### Budget manifold

A limestone and brass manifold divides one federal inlet into annual channels feeding six small
research modules. One inspection valve controls the next channel. This is accurate but risks
reading as energy infrastructure rather than research funding.

### Center mobile

Six architectural research rooms hang from one central budget block. A narrow evidence cable
returns from the rooms through a review lens before it reaches the next budget block. This carries
the partner structure but makes the funding sequence less immediate.

## Selected concept

Five-year stack, one live gate. It makes the difference between an announced total and annual
obligations visible without labels. It also gives the later typography a clean verbal partner.

## Dedup clearance

The style family is translucent resin cross-section. The history ledger forbids the sibling
Texas Desk's generic generated editorial label but contains no resin, museum model, or
exploded-vertical mechanism. The indigo and amber hue family, exploded_vertical composition, and
stacked funding wafer motif are all new.

## Palette

- #08060F, near-black plum for depth and furniture integration
- #17132B, indigo shadow for unactivated years
- #4E5FA8, bluebonnet glass for the suspended wafers
- #EDE6D6, limestone light for the physical report folio
- #C9B393, dust brass for joints and supports
- #E0956A, amber for the active obligation and review aperture

The darkest value sits behind the whole mechanism. The lightest object is the report folio. The
amber aperture has the highest chroma and creates the path from the first funded wafer to the
unlit future stack.

## Composition map

Pattern is exploded_vertical.

- publication quiet band from y 0 to y 190
- main apparatus center near x 545 and y 470
- five resin wafers span x 230 to x 850
- first funded wafer sits near y 610
- review aperture and report folio sit near x 545 and y 500
- four future wafers rise through y 290 to y 455 with controlled separation
- brass guide rails sit near x 250 and x 830
- calm lower headline field begins near y 720
- no essential detail enters the outer 55 pixels

Eye path runs from the warm funded wafer to the pale report folio, through the amber aperture, and
up the cool suspended stack.

## Detail scales

Macro detail is the five-part vertical stack and one bright aperture. Meso detail is the resin
edge thickness, brass mounting rails, physical joins, and the first wafer's warm internal glow.
Micro detail is trapped bubbles in resin, brushed brass wear, paper fibers, tiny fasteners, and
soft museum-case reflections.

## Risks

- The wafers could read as servers. Keep them materially thick, translucent, and specimen-like.
- The report folio could acquire fake writing. Require blank paper with no marks or glyphs.
- The scene could become glossy product advertising. Use restrained museum lighting and tactile
  imperfections.
- The lower stack could collide with the headline. Keep the mechanism's essential aperture above
  y 650 and calm the bottom field.

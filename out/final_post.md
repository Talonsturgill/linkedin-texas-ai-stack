TCEQ data-center permit pause puts the audit at the permit desk.
The September 21st directive ties issuance to ERCOT's review.

This is a hold on state environmental permits for data center projects while agencies gather the information needed to assess them.

- Direction. The Governor's office tells TCEQ to pause permit issuance until ERCOT completes its review and requests a compliance update.
- Impact review. ERCOT collects project information through its State and Community Impact RFI, with utilities submitting developer responses through RIOO.
- Water evidence. TWDB receives the water responses from ERCOT under an arrangement authorized by its Executive Administrator. Respondents need no separate submission to TWDB.
- Permit decision. TCEQ must use the audit information in its permitting work. The letter is addressed to Executive Director Kelly Keel.

The chokepoint is the permit decision. TCEQ can issue a permit or withhold it. The directive requires withholding issuance while ERCOT's review remains unfinished. A developer's completed response does not by itself satisfy that condition. The letter gives no automatic approval once the review ends, and no calendar date when permits must resume.

For Texas AI campuses that need these permits, the machinery links environmental approval to information gathered through the grid audit. Its stated benefit is a better record on power, water and community effects before decisions are made. The tradeoff is a dependency on another agency's review, with no fixed reopening date in the letter. Those are distinct from a finding that a particular campus will cause harm or has met all permit requirements.

The next check is TCEQ's October 19th compliance update to the Governor's office. Look for how it implements the hold and uses audit evidence. That is a reporting deadline, not a promised end to the pause. ERCOT separately requires covered RFI responses by October 12th and expects its impact report by December 10th. Neither date is identified as automatic permit clearance. The permit directive and the RFI also have different scopes, so a project's reporting duties still need to be checked against the actual notice.

The PUCT rulemaking petition gate just closed on a data center metering request.
The Public Utility Commission of Texas denied it in an order dated October 1st, 2026.

A rulemaking petition gate is the step where a state agency must answer a public request for a new rule by denying it in writing or opening a formal rulemaking within 60 days.

The request came from Green Recursive Utility Service LLC in Project No. 60084. It asked the Commission to set a standard for metering computing inside large data centers, which the staff memo describes as particularly those running high-intensity AI workloads. Oncor Electric Delivery Company filed comments taking no position on the legality, merits or need for the relief.

- Statutory petition right. The Texas Legislature wrote Government Code 2001.021 so an interested person can ask an agency for a rule, and the agency then has 60 days to deny in writing with reasons or start a rulemaking.
- Petition form rule. Commission rule 22.281 says a petition must carry complete proposed rule text, and a suggestion that does not is construed as a policy recommendation.
- Staff intake and comment window. Rules and Projects Division staff assigned the petition its own project number and set a comment deadline of September 11th, 2026.
- Staff recommendation. The Division Director memo dated September 25th, 2026 recommended denial. It counted 74 comments in support, said the filing lacked the rule text that rule 22.281 requires, and said nothing in Senate Bill 6 requires the Commission to meter the internal computing of individual data centers.
- Commission order. The signed order denied the petition, the revised petition and any other requested relief.

The chokepoint is the Public Utility Commission of Texas and one binary choice, to deny the petition in writing or initiate a rulemaking proceeding. Staff recommend and commenters argue, but the order is the act that decides. The order gave written reasons. The Commission already has a full slate of Senate Bill 6 rulemaking projects, which the order says contain no requirement to meter computational efficiency, and a new project would redirect staff.

Structural read. The gate turns an open request into a dated answer with written reasons. It does not test whether the idea is right. The order rested on Senate Bill 6 workload and staff resources, and the staff memo added form and authority concerns. This record does not show the internal metering proposal proceeding in another project. One case cannot show whether drafting standards or capacity usually decide these petitions.

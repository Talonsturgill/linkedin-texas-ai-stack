PGRR144 as-built model review puts the machine beside its model.
The ERCOT Board recommended approval on September 15th, 2026. PUCT approval is still pending.

The proposal would make a large computing site's model of its electrical behavior part of the check before the site can take power.

- Rule activation would rest with PUCT, with the revision taking effect on the first day of the month after approval.
- Dynamic model evidence from the facility owner would cover the site's electrical response, including cooling and controls, with converter hardware checks for large computational loads.
- As-built submission by the interconnecting load entity would bring the installed models to ERCOT and its transmission provider, compare them with the study data, and attest that they match field settings.
- As-built review by ERCOT with the transmission provider would check whether changes could harm the prior stability study's results.

The chokepoint is that last review. ERCOT would approve the submission or request more information. Its first response would be due within ten business days, with up to twenty more if needed. A signed statement from the developer would enter a review, not finish it. Approval of this package would still leave other conditions for energization in place.

For AI sites in the ERCOT region, the structural shift is a clearer link between what was studied and what was built. A model of a planned site and a model of the installed equipment would have to meet in the same review. That gives the review a specific object to test before power flows.

The tradeoff is real work. ERCOT says better models help identify reliability issues early. Its impact analysis calls for added review staff. Texas Industrial Energy Consumers argues that extending modeling duties to traditional factories can require outside help and add costs. That objection concerns the proposal's reach beyond computing loads, not proof that a particular AI site is safe or unsafe.
